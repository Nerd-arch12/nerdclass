Your Weekend Eating Schedule May Affect Your Heart

On weekdays, breakfast might be at 8 a.m. and dinner at 7 p.m. But on weekends, many of us sleep longer, have brunch, eat dinner later, and sometimes have late-night snacks.

Research involving more than 100,000 adults in France examined “eating jetlag,” or differences between weekday and weekend meal times. Participants recorded what they consumed and the times of their first and last food or drink. Researchers followed their health for an average of eight years.

Among men, every extra hour of eating jetlag was linked to a 14% higher risk of cardiovascular disease and a 21% higher risk of coronary heart disease. The same link was not found in women. The findings show association rather than proof of causation.

Couples Share Musical Taste More than Personality

Research published in Social Psychology and Personality Science found that romantic partners tended to share more musical preferences than personality traits.

Three studies used questionnaires about music, personality and relationship feelings. Couples shared more musical likes and dislikes with each other than with random participants. Similar personalities did not make couples happier, but a shared favorite musician was linked with slightly greater tenderness.

In the third study, couples reported slightly stronger feelings of love when they shared a most disliked musician. The result was not affected by whether they were musicians.

2. What is the central finding?

67% of Americans Change Their Personality at Work

Express Employment Professionals surveyed 1,000 US job seekers. It found that 67% had changed some part of their personality for work, while 63% said they were different at work and at home.

Younger job seekers reported adaptation more frequently than older respondents. Most considered adaptation important, although many questioned whether personality had anything to do with job performance.

A separate survey of hiring decision-makers found that personality affects perceptions of performance and career advancement.

Advanced Grammar

Hedging

Use may, might, could, appears to and is associated with to avoid claims stronger than the evidence.

Irregular eating causes heart disease. → Irregular eating may be associated with higher risk.

Passive reporting

Use It is believed that... or subject + is believed to + verb.

Researchers believe music influences relationships. → Music is believed to influence relationships.

Inversion

After an initial negative expression, place the auxiliary before the subject.

People rarely question the cost. → Rarely do people question the cost.
